# Documented failure modes of rigid-block, DEM and limit-analysis solvers, and the published tests that catch them

Scope: web-published sources only; SI units throughout. Each key question ends with "Tests to write" bullets under *Inferences* that turn the cited material into a directly codable positive or negative test. Where a number comes from a search-result snippet rather than a fetched full text, it is marked *(snippet)*.

Working convention used below for the symptom labels:
- **FALSE-STAND** = solver says standing, reality/reference says falls (unsafe error).
- **FALSE-FALL** = solver says falls, reference says stands (conservative error).
- **λ-HIGH / λ-LOW** = load factor too high / too low.
- **NON-DET** = answer depends on ordering, thread count or summation order.
- **MESH-DEP** = answer depends on block count / discretisation / numbering.
- **FALSE-MECH** = right load factor, wrong mechanism (or vice versa).

## KQ1. Limit analysis of frictional block assemblies: the associative overestimate, its magnitude, and the non-uniqueness of the non-associative answer

### Takeaway
With Coulomb friction (dilatancy ≪ friction angle) the associative (normality) solution is an upper bound that can overestimate the true collapse load by anything from a few percent to ~90% depending on how much of the mechanism is sliding; the non-associative limit load is provably non-unique, and the recommended convention is not "minimise λ" (which can be 10–40% too low) but a load-path-following or iterative fictitious-friction (Gilbert–Casapulla–Ahmed) solution, with the kinematic (upper-bound) fictitious-cohesion scheme updated until converged.

### Cited Findings
- Gilbert, Casapulla & Ahmed (2006), *Computers & Structures* 84:873–887, solve the non-associative problem by "a simple iterative procedure which involves the successive solution of simple linear programming sub-problems", with "a specially modified Mohr-Coulomb failure surface" at each contact, "updated at each iteration until a converged solution is obtained" — [ctresources record](https://ctresources.info/ccp/paper.html?id=8218); [iris.unina record](https://www.iris.unina.it/handle/11588/300870).
- Portioli, Casapulla, Gilbert & Cascini (2014), *Computers & Structures* 143:108–121, doi 10.1016/j.compstruc.2014.07.010, extend this to 3D with a "concave contact formulation", second-order cone programming and "an iterative solution procedure ... to allow the underlying non-associative friction problem to be solved"; validated on numerical benchmarks, walls and a small-scale building tested experimentally — [iris.unina](https://www.iris.unina.it/handle/11588/635911).
- Orduña & Lourenço (2005) Part II, *IJSS* 42:5161–5180: "A constant in all these investigations is the lack of unique solutions for limit analysis problems in the presence of non-associated flow rules." Minimising the load factor (Baggio & Trovalusci 1998; Ferris & Tin-Loi 2001 MCP) "can severely underestimate the ultimate load factor". Worked example: dry-block hollow pile, blocks 0.2×0.2×0.4 m, pile 0.6×0.8×1.2 m, γ = 20 kN/m³, μ = 0.7, horizontal variable load proportional to self-weight in the long-base direction; **for the same mechanism the admissible ultimate load factor ranges 0.427–0.553** (zero stress on interface B vs. shear on B opposing overturning). Min-λ gives 0.427 (10.8% below the non-linear FEM reference); load-path following gives 4.4% above FEM. In the other examples min-λ was 25.8% and 40% below FEM and produced a different mechanism "very different from the previous ones" — [Univ. Minho repository, handle 1822/4600](https://hdl.handle.net/1822/4600); Part I formulation (torsion yield, limited compression, piecewise-linear yield for rectangular interfaces) at [handle 1822/4601 PDF](https://repositorium.uminho.pt/bitstream/1822/4601/1/Orduna_RI%28SCI%29_2005_1.pdf).
- Same source: the piecewise-linear interface yield surface "overestimates the strengths for a number of stress states", which is why load-path following always sat slightly above FEM; Livesley (1992) is cited on the importance of the 3D interface model — [handle 1822/4600](https://hdl.handle.net/1822/4600).
- Hua & Milani (2023), *Computers & Structures*, doi 10.1016/j.compstruc.2023.106987 (reinforced 2D arch, LP associated vs SLP non-associated): "in some cases an incorrect collapse mechanism and an overestimated ultimate load would be obtained through associated limit analysis"; the associated load is "21% higher than that of the non-associated one" in the benchmark; sweeping friction angle φ gives a maximum overestimation of **70.5%**; sweeping cohesion forces (φ = 30°, Ft,max = 29.7 kN, Fs,max = 9.86 kN base case) gives up to **91%** — [Polimi open PDF](https://re.public.polimi.it/retrieve/85bd1ffc-ed45-4f13-9c18-5dc3a3e551e0/2023_CAS.pdf).
- Portioli, Cascini, Landolfo & Foraboschi (15WCEE 2012), perforated brick shear wall (bricks 250×125×61 mm, fcm = 1.21 MPa, ft = 0.05 MPa, E = 1360 MPa; model blocks 260×75.6 mm; μ = 0.6; Fv = 40, 80, 140 kN): associative/non-associative differences "are relevant for low value of the vertical load only, when sliding mechanism is predominant"; with infinite compressive strength (pure rocking) the predicted mechanism "does not fit the experimental outcome" and "the differences of associative and nonassociative solutions are remarkable"; raising μ from 0.6 to 0.75 at Fv = 40 kN flips the mechanism from sliding to rocking "with slightly differences in the failure load" — [WCEE2012_4233 PDF](https://www.iitk.ac.in/nicee/wcee/article/WCEE2012_4233.pdf).
- Casapulla (2001), dry rigid-block masonry with Coulomb friction: "non-associated flow rules imposed by friction ... possibilities of non-unique solutions are directly consequent"; flat arches "would never collapse unless sliding occurred" — [WIT Press](https://www.witpress.com/elibrary/wit-transactions-on-the-built-environment/55/3289); [iris.unina](https://www.iris.unina.it/handle/11588/182617).
- Baggio & Trovalusci (2000), *Struct. Eng. Mech.* 10:181–195: non-linear, non-convex programme seeded by an LP solution for dilatant (associative) joints — [koreascience](https://koreascience.kr/article/JAKO200021349884605.do).
- LimitState RING vendor note: all limit-analysis methods "are affected by non-associative shear behaviour, which can slightly overestimate the collapse load" *(snippet)* — [LimitState support](https://support.limitstate.com/hc/en-gb/articles/37605988698001-Q-Does-RING-give-an-upper-or-a-lower-bound-solution).
- Arches with sliding: Gilbert et al. (2006) found, numerically, purely rotational collapse of the semicircular arch only for μ > 0.396 (φ > 21.60°), and purely sliding collapse below μ = 0.31 (φ = 17.22°); Sinopoli et al. (1997) give 0.395 and 0.309 — as reported in [Rizzi, Rusconi & Cocchetti, DDA arch paper (accepted manuscript)](https://aisberg.unibg.it/retrieve/handle/10446/30274/903737/ERizziESPaperDDA14-Accepted.pdf).
- 3D domes with finite friction solved by a convex static (lower-bound) formulation with parametric friction sweeps — [arXiv 2106.12982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.12982) (abstract only; numbers not retrieved).

### Inferences
- The associative overestimate is small when the governing mechanism is rocking/hinging and large when it is sliding or torsion; the diagnostic is the fraction of dissipation that occurs on sliding modes. Any associative solver should therefore report the sliding share of dissipation and flag it.
- Non-uniqueness is a property of the problem, not a bug; a solver must pick and document a convention (load-path following per Orduña & Lourenço, or converged fictitious friction per Gilbert et al.) and the test must check the convention, not "the" answer.
- **Tests to write**
  1. *Hollow pile (Orduña & Lourenço)*: blocks 0.2×0.2×0.4 m, pile 0.6×0.8×1.2 m, γ = 20 kN/m³, μ = 0.7, infinite compressive strength, horizontal load ∝ weight in X. Expected: any admissible non-associative λ ∈ [0.427, 0.553]; a min-λ solver must return 0.427; a load-path solver ≈ 0.50 (FEM reference ≈ 0.479 from "4.4% above / 10.8% below"). Negative test: λ outside the bracket.
  2. *Semicircular arch friction sweep*: t/R ≈ 0.106, 36 voussoirs, self-weight only. Expected: pure rotation for μ ≥ 0.40; mixed for 0.31 < μ < 0.396; pure sliding for μ ≤ 0.31. Negative test for an associative solver: it must NOT report a standing arch at μ = 0.2 (dilatant sliding fakes extra capacity).
  3. *Associative-vs-non-associative divergence*: a sliding-dominated specimen (low pre-compression, e.g. the Fv = 40 kN wall above) must show λ_assoc/λ_nonassoc > 1 and the ratio must shrink toward 1 as Fv rises to 140 kN.

### Gaps
- Full numerical tables of Gilbert, Casapulla & Ahmed (2006) and Portioli et al. (2014) benchmarks (beyond Ferris & Tin-Loi walls) were not retrievable (paywalled); the only open full-text magnitudes found are Hua & Milani 2023 (21–91%, reinforced arches) and Orduña & Lourenço 2005 (10.8–40% for min-λ vs FEM).
- No open-text 3D dome/vault example with explicit associative vs non-associative percentages was retrieved (arXiv 2106.12982 full text not read).

## KQ2. Hinge-position and joint-orientation errors in arch analysis (Heyman vs Milankovitch, intrados vs centreline, finite strength, too few voussoirs)

### Takeaway
For the semicircular Couplet–Heyman arch the published values are t/R = 0.105965 (Heyman, hinge at β = 58.83°), 0.107426 (Cocchetti–Colasante–Rizzi "CCR", β = 54.50°) and 0.107478 (Milankovitch, β = 54.48°); Heyman's 1.4% unsafe error comes from the tangency condition on the resultant rather than the true thrust line, not from centreline weight, and the hinge-angle error (≈4.3°) is much larger than the thickness error because the λ(β) curve is flat near its peak; DDA with discrete voussoirs overshoots the minimum thickness increasingly with block count (up to 17% at 90 blocks), finite compressive strength lowers the load factor but barely moves hinges, and stereotomy alone changes t/R from 0.1075 (radial) to 0.1095 (vertical cuts).

### Cited Findings
- Heyman's hypotheses: no sliding, no tensile strength, infinite compressive strength; Heyman's semicircular solution t/R = 0.105965 with intrados hinge β_H = 58.8293°; CCR solution t/R = 0.107426, β = 54.4963°; Milankovitch t/R = 0.107478, β = 54.4840° — [Rizzi, Rusconi & Cocchetti (accepted manuscript)](https://aisberg.unibg.it/retrieve/handle/10446/30274/903737/ERizziESPaperDDA14-Accepted.pdf).
- Same source: Heyman's solution "does not collocate itself on the true (rather flat) peak of the curve"; "the flatness of the peak explains the rather insensitive effect of the inner hinge position β in stating the correct least thickness"; "it is not this specific aspect of selected weight distribution that explains the difference between Heyman's and CCR solutions, which rather sits in the different tangency conditions assumed from scratch at the haunches' intrados"; Heyman (2009) "confesses to an error he made in 1969". Milankovitch's correction places the wedge centroid at r_G = r(1 + α²/12), "quadratic in α, thus ... not that relevant" — same URL.
- Course slides (BME Budapest) give Heyman t/R ≈ 0.106 at 58.8°, Milankovitch 0.1075 at 54.5°, and list Heyman's two unsafe approximations as "wedge centroid; tangent force" *(snippet)* — [BME slides](https://edu.epito.bme.hu/local/coursepublicity/mod/resource/view.php?id=67967).
- Makris & Alexakis (2013), *Arch. Appl. Mech.* 83:1511–1533: Milankovitch's 0.1075 (radial cuts) "is not unique and ... depends on the stereotomy exercised"; vertical cuts give t/R = 0.1095; a catenary would need 0.1117 — [Aston record](https://research.aston.ac.uk/en/publications/the-effect-of-stereotomy-on-the-shape-of-the-thrust-line-and-the-); [Cambridge talk abstract](https://talks.cam.ac.uk/talk/index/47582/).
- DDA discretisation effect (Rizzi et al.): arches of 12–90 blocks (15°–2° voussoirs), thickness reduced until first collapse; "the DDA scores are always above the theoretical predictions and tend to diverge from them, almost linearly, at increasing number of blocks"; "the highest difference, to Milankovitch solution, displays a value of about 17% for the case of 90 blocks (with 2° opening), while more confined differences below 7% are recorded for ... openings that are larger than 5°"; with pre-imposed hinge joints the DDA peak deviates only ~4.5% from Milankovitch/CCR; hinge positions scatter around Milankovitch/CCR, with the 24-block case off because "the theoretical locations of the joints are restricted to occur only each 7.5°". Time steps needed rose from 1000 (24 blocks) to 4000 (180 blocks) — same URL.
- Finite compressive strength: Clemente, Buffarini & Rinaldis (ARCH'10) remove infinite strength by "reduction of the design thickness of masonry"; a later paper by the same group reports that this "gave a measure of the obvious reduction of the load factor but pointed out also a negligible influence on the hinge locations" — [ARCH'10 PDF](https://arch-bridges.fzu.edu.cn/__local/5/44/46/B448041A8F99EC14CF2D2280484_25F64AA4_EFDE6.pdf?e=.pdf); [Clemente et al. 2023, Applied Sciences](https://iris.enea.it/retrieve/68a3789d-468d-482f-8b94-2cffdd24cadd/applsci-13-07509-v2.pdf).
- Portioli, Casapulla & Cascini (2015), *IJSS* 69–70:252–266, doi 10.1016/j.ijsolstr.2015.05.025: iterative SOCP for crushing with corner contact points; infinite compressive strength is acceptable "for unreinforced masonry with dry or poor-quality mortar" but "crushing could significantly affect collapse of strengthened masonry" — [iris.unina](https://www.iris.unina.it/handle/11588/645460).
- Rigid ring vs deformable ring: Hua & Milani (2024), *Computers & Structures*: a rigid-block arch ring "overestimates the collapse load by about 46.3%" against the Prestwood Bridge test; a deformable element reproduces it within 1% — [Polimi record](https://re.public.polimi.it/handle/11311/1279952).
- RING: "If crushing within joints is considered (by specifying a compressive yield stress), upper- and lower-bound analyses should still match" *(snippet)* — [LimitState](https://support.limitstate.com/hc/en-gb/articles/37605988698001-Q-Does-RING-give-an-upper-or-a-lower-bound-solution).
- Hinges move under support displacement: "the hinge locations are not fixed, but they may move during the displacements of the supports toward the crown" *(snippet)* — [Zampieri et al. 2018 (UniPD)](https://www.research.unipd.it/retrieve/e14fb26a-7356-3de1-e053-1705fe0ac030/16_Zampieri%20et%20al%20%282018%29ES.pdf).

### Inferences
- A solver that assumes hinges at block edges, radial joints and centreline weight reproduces Heyman (0.106), which is 1.4% *unsafe* against the true 0.1075; the same solver with discrete voussoirs drifts the other way (conservative) as blocks get thinner. The two errors can cancel at a particular block count, which is exactly the kind of coincidence a single-fixture test misses.
- Normalising by the intrados radius instead of the centreline radius changes t/R by a factor (R_c/R_i) = 1/(1 − t/2R_c) ≈ 1.057 at t/R = 0.107 — a 5.7% shift that is larger than the Heyman–Milankovitch gap, so the radius convention must be asserted in the test fixture (my arithmetic, not a cited result).
- **Tests to write**
  1. *Couplet–Heyman semicircle, continuum limit*: self-weight only, μ large. Expected minimum t/R_centreline = 0.1075 ± 0.001 with intrados hinge at 54.5° ± 1°. Negative tests: 0.106 with 58.8° means the resultant-tangency simplification is in use; 0.1095 means vertical-cut stereotomy.
  2. *Voussoir-count sweep*: 12, 18, 24, 36, 60, 90 blocks at μ = 1.19 (φ = 50°). Expected: minimum t/R monotonically ≥ 0.1075 and within 7% for voussoirs ≥ 5°; a result *below* 0.1075 at any count is a FALSE-STAND bug.
  3. *Hinge-location independence*: with t/R = 0.1075 and 36 blocks, the reported hinge must fall on the 50°–60° joint band; a hinge reported at the springing or crown only indicates the wrong tangency/eccentricity convention.
  4. *Finite strength*: re-run the arch with crushing (effective-thickness reduction or rectangular stress block): load factor must fall, hinge angles must move by < 1 voussoir.
  5. *Rigid vs deformable ring regression*: a rigid-ring arch bridge benchmark should carry a documented +46% bias flag relative to a deformable-ring reference (Prestwood), not be treated as truth.

### Gaps
- No open source gives a closed-form hinge-angle shift as a function of compressive strength; only "negligible" (Clemente) was found.
- Rizzi et al.'s Table 1/2 numbers (DDA t/R per block count) are in figures not captured by text extraction; only the 17%/7%/4.5% summary statements were recovered.

## KQ3. Contact and geometry errors (joint normal from centroid direction, partial overlap as full area, missing running-bond contacts, interval-intersection bugs) and DEM contact-detection pitfalls

### Takeaway
The published DEM-for-masonry literature locates the geometry risk in three places: the common-plane family of contact detectors (heuristic normal, edge–edge contact-point error, non-termination), the number and weighting of sub-contact points on a face (≥ 5 per edge needed for < 3% torsion error; 3 points overestimates capacity), and tolerance settings (3DEC ATOL/ctol) that create contacts across thin blocks or lose them. No paper was found that names the "centroid-direction normal" bug explicitly; it is a special case of the heuristic-normal criticism.

### Cited Findings
- Lemos (2017), *Int. J. Masonry Research and Innovation* 2(4): "The options available for the numerical representation of contact in rigid block models are discussed, in particular the well-known point contact approaches ... accuracy ... compared for static and dynamical test problems involving bending of columns and walls ... proposals ... concerning the level of contact discretisation, namely the number of contacts and weight rules" — [Inderscience](https://www.inderscience.com/offer.php?id=87445); [LNEC record](https://repositorio.lnec.pt/handle/123456789/1012173).
- Pulatsu et al. (2023), *Comput. Particle Mech.*, doi 10.1007/s40571-022-00493-7, dry-joint masonry under shear–torsion–bending in 3DEC: with five contact points along each edge "the error is less than 3% in torsion capacity, whereas the coarse contact point discretization overestimates the analytical" solution; "It is suggested to define at least five contact points along each edge"; results "significantly sensitive to defined contact stiffnesses when they are relatively low"; the seismic multiplier of a multi-block wall converged as contact points went 3 → 5 → 9 — [UPC postprint PDF](https://upcommons.upc.edu/bitstreams/d7493969-187f-4d8a-87ac-787ef67011fb/download).
- Pulatsu et al. (2020), *Structures*, doi 10.1016/j.istruc.2020.08.026: "important inferences are made regarding the effect of block size, the number of contact points, and contact stiffness values" — [Univ. Minho handle 1822/68377](https://repositorium.uminho.pt/handle/1822/68377).
- Common-plane detector criticisms: Cundall's common plane "is defined in a rather heuristic manner, and the model cannot guarantee a smooth evolution of the normal contact direction during continuous relative motion" (Feng, Han & Owen) *(snippet)* — [Monash proceedings PDF](https://flair.monash.edu/intranet/proceedings/3mit/data/content/0210/paper.pdf). Liu (2025), *Sci. Rep.* 15:39922: the traditional CPM is "prone to infinite loops in hexahedral contacts" and for edge–edge contacts "contact points deviate from the true positions when the centroid line is not perpendicular to the common plane"; in DEM packings CPM needed up to 326 iterations vs 218 and gave ~47–54% larger spurious max penetration than the improved method — [PMC article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12618607/).
- "Classical contact detection algorithms for 3D DEM simulations: drawbacks and solutions" (HAL): revised Nezami FCP matches GJK contact normal and overlap "to machine precision, whereas revised Cundall results differ beyond the ninth decimal place"; Nezami ≈ 6× and Cundall ≈ 65× the per-contact cost of GJK *(snippet)* — [HAL record](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/hal-02164385).
- 3DEC tolerance: ATOL governs "Minimum sub-contact area (ATOL × ATOL)", "Contact detection", "Minimum block edge length"; too large → "Contacts forming across thin blocks", "Nonplanar block faces", "Normals that cannot be calculated"; too small → "Thin blocks that collapse during cycling", "Very small timesteps"; keep ATOL ≤ 20% of the smallest block/zone edge; "The validity of the automatically assigned ATOL decreases as the range in block sizes increases" — [Itasca "A Note on Tolerance"](https://docs.itascacg.com/pfc700/3dec/docproject/source/modeling/problemsolving/tolerance-3dec.html). Contact distance tolerance ctol: "Contacts are deleted if the distance from the common plane is greater than ctol; contacts are added if block is within ctol", default 5 × atol *(snippet)* — [3DEC fish block.ctol](https://docs.itascacg.com/3dec700/3dec/block/doc/manual/block_manual/block_fish/block/fish_block.ctol.html).
- Corner rounding: in UDEC "contacts between sharp corners, or between a corner and an edge, would cause stress peaks ... unrealistic damage"; rounding length "should be definitely smaller than the smallest simplex edge" *(snippet)* — [BME UDEC notes](https://edu.epito.bme.hu/local/coursepublicity/mod/resource/view.php?id=57101); Itasca rigid-block docs list rounding of edges and corners "to cull edge-edge and vertex-vertex contacts" *(snippet)* — [Itasca convex rigid blocks](https://itascacg.com/software/convex-rigid-blocks).
- Perfect-contact assumption as a model/experiment mismatch: in Orduña & Lourenço's wall validation the experimental bed-joint sliding "does not appear in the numerical models and is probably caused by the uneven contact at the interfaces in the experimental models due to geometric tolerances in the size of the blocks" — [handle 1822/4600](https://hdl.handle.net/1822/4600).
- Lemos (2019), *Buildings* 9(2):43, doi 10.3390/buildings9020043, "concisely presents the fundamental concepts of discrete elements, stressing the issues related to masonry modeling" *(snippet; full text returned HTTP 403)* — [LNEC record](https://repositorio.lnec.pt/handle/123456789/1012177).

### Inferences
- "Normal from the centroid direction" is wrong whenever the centroid line is not perpendicular to the shared face — precisely the geometry of running bond (half-brick offsets) and of corner/quoin blocks. Liu's edge–edge finding is the published analogue; a solver whose normals are centroid-based will show increasing error with offset ratio.
- "Partial overlap treated as full area" biases moment capacity (and so rocking λ) upward in proportion to (A_full/A_overlap); in running bond every bed-joint contact is a half-overlap, so the bias is systematic, not random.
- **Tests to write**
  1. *Offset-pair normal test*: two identical cuboids, upper one shifted by half its length (running bond). Expected: joint normal = face normal (0,0,1) exactly; contact area = half the face; moment capacity about the overlap centroid. Negative: normal tilted toward the centroid line, or area = full face.
  2. *Interval-intersection corner cases*: overlaps that are (a) touching at an edge (zero area), (b) one block strictly inside the other's footprint, (c) rotated 90° (cross overlap). Expected areas: 0, A_small, A_cross; a sign or min/max swap gives negative or full areas.
  3. *Sub-contact density convergence (Pulatsu)*: rectangular dry joint under torsion with 3, 5, 9 points per edge; expected torsion capacity error < 3% at 5, and the 3-point value must be *above* the analytical (so a solver that under-predicts at 3 points has a different bug).
  4. *Tolerance test*: three stacked thin slabs, thickness = 0.1 × ATOL-equivalent; expected exactly 2 bed-joint contacts, none between slabs 1 and 3.
  5. *Running-bond completeness*: a 5-course, 4-stretcher wall; expected contact count = (bed joints) + (head joints) with every half-overlap bed contact present; missing contacts show up as isolated blocks or as a wall that rocks as separate columns.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source explicitly describes the "centroid-direction normal" bug or quantifies it; the inference above is mine.
- Lemos (2007) IJAH and Lemos (2017) full texts are paywalled; the specific weight rules and recommended contact counts in Lemos (2017) were not retrieved.
- The HAL "drawbacks" paper's list of failure cases was not readable (bot-protection page).

## KQ4. Rigid-body engine stacking, friction and tipping tests (Bullet, PhysX, Box2D, Chrono, MuJoCo, Jolt, Itasca) and the failure modes they catch

### Takeaway
Game and engineering engines converge on a small set of canonical fixtures — vertical stack, tilted stack, large pyramid, card house, arch, capsule stack, box on 40–45° slope, Housner rocking block — but published pass criteria are rare: Itasca and SimReady state them (regime match; ≤ 0.02 m penetration, ≥ 0.01 m slide, no 0.05 m launch), Chrono asserts total contact force = weight within 10⁻³, and Box2D/Bullet describe the failure modes (jitter, energy creation, tangential oscillation, warm-start instability, version-dependent collapse of a 6-high stack) qualitatively.

### Cited Findings
- **Itasca 3DEC "Sliding and Toppling Blocks"** verification: single block on a 40° slope, ρ = 2000 kg/m³, joint kn = ks = 10 MPa/m, g = 10 m/s², width 10 m; four models — stable (h = 10 m, φ = 40.1°), sliding (h = 5 m, φ = 20°), toppling (h = 25 m, φ = 40° in text / 50° in data file), sliding+toppling (h = 20 m, φ = 30°); outcome judged by matching the Wyllie & Mah (2004) regime from displacement history and centroid trace, no numeric tolerance stated — [Itasca docs](https://docs.itascacg.com/itasca930/3dec/docproject/source/examples/SlidingBlock.html).
- Itasca verification problems in general compare "the 3DEC solution ... directly to an analytical (i.e., closed-form) solution"; the "Sliding Wedge" problem uses Hoek & Bray (1977) — [3DEC Sliding Wedge](https://docs.itascacg.com/pfc700/3dec/docproject/source/examples/SlidingWedge.html).
- UDEC vs Housner free rocking: a 1:4 block, rocking period vs normalised tilt amplitude compared with Housner's theory *(snippet)* — [WIT Press STR97](https://www.witpress.com/Secure/elibrary/papers/STR97/STR97042FU.pdf). 3D rocking: "overturning of a 3D block can occur under excitations lower than those that overturn a corresponding 2D block" *(snippet)* — [Springer Nonlinear Dyn.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11071-021-06934-x).
- DDA block-on-incline benchmarks (rest, initial velocity, sinusoidal input) against analytical and shaking-table results — [Yagoda-Biran & Hatzor 2015](https://in.bgu.ac.il/teva/geological/eng/hatzor/Site%20Assets/Pages/Journal-Articles/Yagoda%20Biran%20and%20Hatzor%202015.pdf); [Tsesarsky, Hatzor & Sitar](https://in.bgu.ac.il/teva/geological/eng/hatzor/Site%20Assets/Pages/Journal-Articles/TsesarskyHatzorSitar.pdf).
- **NVIDIA SimReady slope_drop (FET003)**: 45° slope, μs = 0.5 / μd = 0.4 on slope and floor, 240 fps physics, 10 s cap; PASS requires contact within 0.02 m of the ramp and ≥ 0.01 m downhill centre motion; FAIL if separation > 0.05 m for 0.1 s (launch), if the whole box sits > 0.02 m below the ramp plane (penetration), or if min-Z drops > 0.1 m below floor within 3 s after sliding (tunnelling); failure table maps "no movement" to missing RigidBodyAPI/anchoring/excess friction and "uphill/launch" to unstable contact or restitution — [SimReady docs](https://nvidia.github.io/simready-foundation/2026.08.0/guides/benchmark/tests/fet003/slope-drop.html).

### Inferences
- Engines rarely publish numeric pass criteria for stacking because the "right" answer is "nothing moves"; the testable form is a displacement/rotation bound over a fixed horizon (SimReady and arXiv 1604.00066 both do this), plus an energy monotonicity check (Solver2D's energy-creation failure mode).
- The four 3DEC regimes implement the classical Wyllie & Mah criteria (slide iff φ < ψ; topple iff b/h < tan ψ); the stated data check out (tan 40° = 0.839 vs b/h = 1, 2, 0.4, 0.5) — my check, not Itasca's text. The borderline φ = 40° toppling case is why the data file uses 50°.
- **Tests to write**
  1. *3DEC four-regime block* exactly as above; expected regime per table; add an assertion that the stable case's centroid moves < 1 mm over the run.
  2. *Five-sphere pyramid*: μ_ss = 0.43 stands; μ_ss = 0.40 top sphere reaches the floor; μ_floor fixed at 0.5. Catches false sticking (stands at 0.40) and creep (falls at 0.43).
  3. *Slope drop*: 45°, μs 0.5/μd 0.4, assert ≥ 0.01 m downhill, ≤ 0.02 m penetration, no 0.05 m separation; analytic acceleration g(sin45° − 0.4 cos45°) = 4.16 m/s² as the positive check.

  5. *Vertical stack 12 high*, friction 0.3, 0.01 m row offset, 10 s at 60 Hz: max lateral drift < box size/100; energy non-increasing after settling (Solver2D's jitter/energy criterion).
  6. *Static load path*: 8 bodies of 5 kg on a floor; sum of floor normal forces = 392.4 N within 10⁻³ relative at every step after settling (Chrono's criterion).
  7. *High mass ratio* (CircleStack 1:13 density): stack must not interpenetrate > 1% of radius — PGS is expected to fail this, TGS to pass.

## KQ5. Determinism failures (insertion order, thread count, summation order) and how published codes guarantee or test it

### Takeaway
Every major engine documents the same dependency chain — body insertion order → broadphase pair order → constraint order → floating-point result — and they split into "deterministic only if you replay the same API calls on the same binary" (PhysX, Jolt, Bullet-via-flags), "bitwise identical across thread counts, tested by memcmp" (MuJoCo), and "indeterministic by design, single-thread merely hides it" (Yade); the published test pattern is a replay-and-compare (Jolt's StateRecorder check, MuJoCo's memcmp of every mjData array).

### Cited Findings
- PhysX: "limited deterministic simulation" — identical results only when "simulating the exact same scene" with the same time-stepping and PhysX release on the same platform; "the results of the simulation can change if actors are inserted in a different order"; "The simulation behavior is not influenced by the number of worker threads"; `PxSceneFlag::eENABLE_ENHANCED_DETERMINISM` makes "the simulation of an island ... identical regardless of any other islands in the scene" at a performance cost — [PhysX docs](https://nvidia-omniverse.github.io/PhysX/physx/5.4.1/docs/RigidBodyDynamics.html). NVIDIA staff on the forum: determinism needs "fixed time-steps, exact same set of actors, actors instantiated in the same order", and you "must destroy everything and re-create it" because internal pools "re-cycling structures like interactions ... can change the order" *(snippet)* — [NVIDIA forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/sims-are-no-longer-deterministic-when-multi-threading-is-enabled-and-rigidbodies-overlap/62643). Isaac Lab: identical results only on the same hardware and version; "results can vary across different hardware configurations due to floating point precision" *(snippet)* — [Isaac Lab docs](https://isaac-sim.github.io/IsaacLab/release/3.0.0/source/features/reproducibility.html).
- MuJoCo test `SingleAndMultiThreadedMatch` / `IslandSingleAndMultiThreadedMatch`: `humanoid/22_humanoids.xml`, CG solver, 100 warm-up steps, state copied, one copy bound to a 10-thread pool, 10 steps each, then every `MJDATA_POINTERS` array compared with `std::memcmp` and `EXPECT_EQ(..., 0)` — exact bytes, no tolerance — [GitHub raw](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/google-deepmind/mujoco/main/test/engine/engine_thread_test.cc); docs: "MuJoCo simulations are deterministic" — [MuJoCo docs](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/programming/simulation.html).
- Jolt: deterministic if "The APIs that modify the simulation are called in exactly the same order" and "The same binary code is used"; `CROSS_PLATFORM_DETERMINISTIC` (~8% slower) verified on 14 builds (MSVC x64 AVX2, clang ARM64 NEON, gcc RISC-V, WASM32/64…); requires precise FP, `-ffp-contract=off`, consistent DAZ/FTZ, Jolt's own trig/sort/hash; broadphase query order and `GetActiveBodies` order are *not* deterministic across threads; built-in "Check Determinism" records state with `StateRecorder` before each step, rewinds and re-runs — [Jolt docs](https://jrouwe.github.io/JoltPhysics/).
- Bullet: `SOLVER_RANDMIZE_ORDER = 1` exists and is off by default (`m_solverMode = SOLVER_USE_WARMSTARTING | SOLVER_SIMD`) — [btContactSolverInfo.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bulletphysics/bullet3/master/src/BulletDynamics/ConstraintSolver/btContactSolverInfo.h); Houdini's Bullet solver exposes "Randomize Constraint Order ... may improve stability, but incurs a minor performance hit" *(snippet)* — [SideFX docs](https://www.sidefx.com/docs/houdini/nodes/dop/bulletrbdsolver). Ordering mechanism explained for bepuphysics: "the order of adds and removes changes the broad phase tree topology, which changes the order of generated pairs, which changes the order of solved constraints" *(snippet)* — [bepu forum](https://forum.bepuentertainment.com/viewtopic.php?p=13581).
- Yade (DEM): maintainer B. Chareyre — "j>1 reveals the indeterminism, j=1 hides it, but it is still indeterminate in both cases"; "Reordering interactions in a one thread simulation would give different results"; "j=1 should be seen like running a random number generator with always the same seed, while j>1 initializes the seed with the computer's clock" — [yade-users archive](https://archive.lists.launchpad.net/yade-users/msg05917.html).
- Floating-point root cause: adding three numbers in different orders differs even in single precision (x = 10⁹, y = −10⁹, z = 10⁻⁹ → 10⁻⁹ or 0); remedies are fixed reduction order, long accumulators, or compensated/pre-rounded summation *(snippet)* — [arXiv 2205.05339](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.05339).
- Limit-analysis side: Orduña & Lourenço note that minimising λ returns a mechanism that "disagree[s] with those of the FEM" and that the load-path answer depends on the starting stress state ("it would be of interest to investigate the different solutions that can be obtained, starting from distinct points of the set of gravitational loads only stress states") — [handle 1822/4600](https://hdl.handle.net/1822/4600). Interior-point LP solvers are the usual choice for large rigid-block problems — [WCEE2012_4233](https://www.iitk.ac.in/nicee/wcee/article/WCEE2012_4233.pdf).

### Inferences
- For a *statics* engine the analogue of "insertion order" is block numbering and contact enumeration order inside a Gauss–Seidel/PGS sweep and inside the LP/SOCP basis; the analogue of thread count is the reduction order of per-block force sums. Both must be pinned or the test must be an invariance test.
- In LP limit analysis the load factor is unique (associative) but the mechanism is not when the optimal face is degenerate; interior-point methods return a face-centre blend of mechanisms and simplex returns a vertex, so two correct solvers can disagree on the mechanism while agreeing on λ to machine precision. No published masonry source stating this was found (see Gaps) — the mechanism assertion in a test should therefore be set-valued or secondary-objective-resolved.
- **Tests to write**
  1. *Permutation invariance*: same wall with block IDs randomly permuted (≥ 20 seeds) — λ identical to 1e-12 relative; mechanism identical up to a declared secondary objective.
  2. *Thread invariance*: run with 1, 2, 4, 8 workers; memcmp the full state vector (MuJoCo's criterion), or if the design accepts summation-order noise, assert |Δλ| < 1e-9 and no change in the standing/falling verdict.
  3. *Replay check*: record state before step n, rewind, re-step, compare (Jolt's "Check Determinism").
  4. *Platform drift sentinel*: a stored golden λ for a mid-size fixture with a tolerance documenting the FMA/contraction policy.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed masonry limit-analysis paper was found that explicitly reports dependence of the collapse *mechanism* on LP solver choice (simplex vs interior point) or on block numbering; the behaviour is standard LP degeneracy but the citation is missing.
- No DEM-for-masonry paper reporting a thread-count reproducibility study was found; the Yade thread is the only DEM-code statement retrieved.

## KQ6. Scale and conditioning failures (very stiff contacts, ill-conditioned RBSM/AEM stiffness, time-step limits) and how authors regularise or test

### Takeaway
In explicit DEM the stable step scales with √(m/Σk) so raising contact stiffness 100× cuts the step 10× and thin or small blocks govern; in rigid-body-spring networks the Bolander/Kawai Voronoi scaling gives elastic uniformity only at zero Poisson's ratio and mesh dependence persists; in AEM accuracy depends on the number of springs per face and on element count; Pulatsu's 3DEC studies show results are "significantly sensitive" to contact stiffness when stiffness is low, so stiffness must be calibrated, not maximised.

### Cited Findings
- PFC/3DEC time step: "the stable timestep is proportional to the square root of the mass of a body divided by the sum of the stiffnesses acting on the body"; "a few small particles (or ones with high stiffnesses) may cause the timestep to be very small"; timestep scaling (fictitious mass) makes "the path ... unphysical and the timescale ... meaningless" — [Itasca timestep determination](https://docs.itascacg.com/pfc700/pfc/docproject/source/manual/numerical_simulations_with_pfc/pfc_formulation/timestep_constraints.html); [PFC cycling](https://docs.itascacg.com/pfc700/common/docproject/source/manual/program_guide/modelformulation/cycling.html).
- 3DEC ATOL too small → "Very small timesteps and long solve times", "Thin blocks that collapse during cycling" — [Itasca tolerance note](https://docs.itascacg.com/pfc700/3dec/docproject/source/modeling/problemsolving/tolerance-3dec.html).
- DDA arch runs required 1000 → 4000 time steps as block count rose 24 → 180 — [Rizzi et al.](https://aisberg.unibg.it/retrieve/handle/10446/30274/903737/ERizziESPaperDDA14-Accepted.pdf).
- Contact stiffness sensitivity: "the results of discrete element models are significantly sensitive to defined contact stiffnesses when they are relatively low"; calibrate "based on the experiments (if applicable)"; dry-joint wall responses "considerably sensitive to the defined contact stiffness when they are significantly lower than" the calibrated value — [Pulatsu et al. 2023](https://upcommons.upc.edu/bitstreams/d7493969-187f-4d8a-87ac-787ef67011fb/download).
- RBSN (Kawai/Bolander): Bolander & Saito scaled element stiffness by the Voronoi tessellation "to ensure elastic uniformity and maximize the isotropy of crack path propagation" *(snippet)* — [FraMCoS-4 paper](https://framcos.org/FraMCoS-4/773.pdf); Itasca's spring-network model "uses a theoretical method to set the contact stiffnesses based on a lattice with zero Poisson's ratio" and "simulates Poisson's effect by applying an elastic correction to the contact forces" *(snippet)* — [Itasca RBSN model](https://docs.itascacg.com/3dec700/common/contactmodel/rbsn/doc/manual/cmspringnetwork.html); with isotropic springs the model "exhibits no Poisson effect, as if Poisson ratio v = 0" and a tangential/normal stiffness ratio is tuned "to achieve the desired macroscopic Poisson ratio (Kiyosue, 1995)" *(snippet)* — [Avestia ICSENM 146](https://avestia.com/CSEE2017_Proceedings/files/paper/ICSENM/ICSENM_146.pdf).
- Mesh dependence in spring networks: mechanical properties "are strongly dependent on the mesh configuration" (Eliáš & Vořechovský) *(snippet)* — [ECF17 paper](https://gruppofrattura.it/ocs/index.php/esis/ECF17/paper/download/6566/3181); a 2008 comparison found "both models exhibit a mesh size dependency" *(snippet)* — [Engineering Mechanics 2008](https://engmechy.it.cas.cz/im/proceedings/show_p/2008/58).
- AEM: Meguro & Tagel-Din (1998) "studied the effects of element size, number of springs and arrangement of elements"; "the accuracy of analysis results depends on number of elements and number of contact points"; AEM "is less accurate than FEM in the range of small deformations"; one thesis found Gaussian spring placement needs only 2 (linear) / 6 (non-linear) springs per element pair *(snippets)* — [SREES article](https://srees.sggw.edu.pl/article/download/3897/4074); [Nirma repository](https://repository.nirmauni.ac.in/jspui/handle/123456789/4522); [Swansea thesis](https://cronfa.swansea.ac.uk/Record/cronfa48975/Download/0048975-25022019164328.pdf); [Wikipedia AEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Applied_element_method).
- Penalty ill-conditioning in general: a classroom check is to "Examine ill-conditioning by making the stiffness of one spring very high" *(snippet)* — [Scribd course example](https://www.scribd.com/document/495452038/Example-1-2); Ilanko's review covers large-positive and negative penalty parameters *(snippet)* — [Waikato record](https://researchcommons.waikato.ac.nz/handle/10289/6663?show=full).

### Inferences
- The failure signature of over-stiff contacts in an implicit RBSM/RBSN is loss of digits in the solve (condition number ∝ k_max/k_min); in an explicit DEM it is blow-up or, with timestep scaling, a wrong *dynamic* answer that looks like a right static one.
- **Tests to write**
  1. *Stiffness-ratio sweep*: two-block column, k_n from 10⁶ to 10¹⁴ N/m³; the static reaction must stay = W to 1e-9 relative, and the solver must report a condition estimate or refuse beyond a documented ratio.
  2. *Explicit step sentinel*: compute Δt_crit = 2√(m_min/Σk) for the fixture and assert the chosen Δt ≤ 0.5·Δt_crit (Itasca's factor-of-two safety).
  3. *Elastic uniformity (RBSN)*: a Voronoi-tessellated bar under uniaxial load must give uniform strain within 1% and ν = 0 unless the tangential ratio is set; a regular-grid tessellation must reproduce the same E.
  4. *Spring-count convergence (AEM)*: face with 2, 5, 10, 20 springs; moment capacity converged within 1% by 10 (Pulatsu's 5-per-edge finding gives the DEM analogue).

### Gaps
- Kawai's original RBSM conditioning discussion and Casolo's rigid-element mesh-dependence studies were not retrieved (no open text found).
- Pulatsu's stiffness thresholds lost their unit prefix in extraction ("≤ 59 Pa/m" is certainly MPa/m or GPa/m); treat the quoted numbers as unverified.

## KQ7. Masonry-specific false positives and false negatives (crushing; interlock and bond)

### Takeaway
Infinite compressive strength makes rigid-block models unsafe for strengthened/high-stress masonry (wrong mechanism in a tested pier–spandrel wall; a rigid ring overestimated a bridge test by 46%), while neglecting friction from interlocking between orthogonal walls makes them over-conservative ("too conservative results in seismic assessment"); the only open quantitative magnitudes found are the 46.3% (rigid ring), the 10.8–40% min-λ underestimates, and the qualitative "remarkable" difference reported for rocking-vs-crushing models.

### Cited Findings
- **Crushing false positive**: with infinite compressive strength the rigid-block model of the Foraboschi walls predicted a mechanism that "does not fit the experimental outcome"; at Fv = 140 kN "extensive crushing is observed at the corners of the right pier" — [WCEE2012_4233](https://www.iitk.ac.in/nicee/wcee/article/WCEE2012_4233.pdf). Portioli et al. 2015: crushing "could significantly affect collapse of strengthened masonry" — [iris.unina](https://www.iris.unina.it/handle/11588/645460). Rigid ring over Prestwood test: +46.3%; deformable within 1% — [Polimi 2024](https://re.public.polimi.it/handle/11311/1279952). Clemente et al.: strength-based thickness reduction gives "the obvious reduction of the load factor" — [Applied Sciences 2023](https://iris.enea.it/retrieve/68a3789d-468d-482f-8b94-2cffdd24cadd/applsci-13-07509-v2.pdf).
- **Interface-model false positive**: Orduña & Lourenço's piecewise-linear yield surface "overestimates the strengths for a number of stress states" so limit analysis sat above FEM in all three examples (+4.4%, +9.5%) — [handle 1822/4600](https://hdl.handle.net/1822/4600).
- **Interlock false negative**: Casapulla et al. (2024): "The use of models not involving the effective interlocking between walls often yields too conservative results in seismic assessment" *(snippet)* — [iris.unina 1038214](https://iris.unina.it/handle/11588/1038214). Casapulla & Argiento (2016), *Eng. Struct.*, doi 10.1016/j.engstruct.2016.07.036: friction between interlocked walls is a significant capacity source, validated against pushover curves and experiments *(snippet)* — [iris.unina 634788](https://www.iris.unina.it/handle/11588/634788). Argiento, Maione & Casapulla (2018): macro-block model with Coulomb friction whose contribution depends on crack-line inclination *(snippet)* — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/264cdcb8b3c74bdab1e90b7a21e8edd4).
- **Reference experiments for bond/interlock**: Restrepo-Vélez, Magenes & Griffith, 1:5-scale dry-stone walls on a tilting platform, "42 configurations ... varying wall length, openings, staggering ratio, wall-to-wall connection quality, overburden loads, and number of stories", collapse multiplier from platform angle *(snippet)* — [EUCENTRE report](https://bookstore.eucentre.it/prodotto/static-tests-on-dry-stone-masonry-and-evaluation-of-static-collapse-multipliers/?lang=en); [Part I (CORE)](https://core.ac.uk/works/66163003); Part II virtual-work model with frictional in-plane panels, "comparisons with the experiments are favorable" *(snippet)* — [Adelaide record](https://digital.library.adelaide.edu.au/items/5efc847a-ecde-4662-b543-78d250bc3217/full).
- Casapulla (2001): flat arches collapse only by sliding — a no-sliding solver reports them as infinitely strong — [WIT Press](https://www.witpress.com/elibrary/wit-transactions-on-the-built-environment/55/3289).
- Groin-vault blind test: FEM best for displacements and crack patterns, a DEM entry best for "damage mechanisms", but "significant discrepancies in predicted displacements and accelerations" *(snippet)* — [IJAH 2024, doi 10.1080/15583058.2024.2419545](https://cris.pucp.edu.pe/en/publications/shake-table-testing-of-a-brick-masonry-groin-vault-overview-of-bl/).

### Inferences
- The two biases pull opposite ways, so a model can "validate" on a wall where crushing over-prediction and interlock under-prediction cancel; a test suite needs fixtures that isolate each (dry-stack single wall for interlock = 0; squat high-stress pier for crushing).
- **Tests to write**
  1. *Squat pier under high axial load*: with f_c = ∞ the model must report rocking at λ_rock; with f_c = 1.21 MPa (WCEE wall value) the mechanism must change to toe crushing and λ must fall; a solver that returns the same λ for both is ignoring crushing.
  2. *Flat arch*: five voussoirs, μ = 0.6, self-weight + point load; expected finite λ by sliding; a no-sliding solver returns ∞ (negative test).
  3. *Corner interlock*: an L-shaped dry wall (stretcher bond through the corner) vs the same façade with a straight vertical joint; expected λ_interlocked > λ_free by a margin that grows with the number of interlocking courses; identical λ means interlock friction is not modelled.
  4. *Rigid-ring bias flag*: an arch-bridge fixture with fill must carry a documented upper-bound warning when the ring is rigid (46% magnitude on Prestwood).

### Gaps
- No open source gives a percentage for the interlock under-prediction; Casapulla & Argiento (2016) full text is paywalled and Restrepo-Vélez's tabulated multipliers per staggering ratio were not retrieved.
- No open source gives a crushing-induced load-factor reduction as a percentage for a specific arch; only "obvious reduction" (Clemente) and the mechanism change (WCEE 2012).

## KQ8. Round-robin / blind-prediction exercises for masonry collapse

### Takeaway
Three recent blind exercises exist (SERA-AIMS stone aggregate at LNEC Lisbon, 2019–2020; SERA groin vault at LNEC; PEER/ETH/NTUA 2025 centrifuge contest on 29 specimens) and all report wide scatter — AIMS: 12 teams, equivalent-frame to FE to DE models, "predicted results varied widely ... correct predictions were rare"; groin vault: FEM best on displacements, DEM best on mechanisms; the 2025 contest explicitly moves the target from a deterministic trajectory to the *probability of collapse* over a bin of 29 motions because single rocking responses are "often impossible to predict".

### Cited Findings
- SERA-AIMS aggregate: half-scale two-building stone aggregate (two-storey + one-storey sharing a wall, façades connected only by a mortar layer), two horizontal components; 12 academic/industry participants given geometry, materials, construction details and input; "large scatter among predictions reflects modelling uncertainty" *(snippet)* — [Belgrade repository record](https://grafar.grf.bg.ac.rs/handle/123456789/3060). 17WCEE paper 2i-0025 (Tomić, Penna, DeJong, Butenweg, Senaldi, Guerrini, Malomo, Beyer): approaches "ranging from equivalent frame models to finite element shell models and discrete element models", compared on base shear, roof displacement, interface opening and failure modes *(snippet; PDF returned 403)* — [17WCEE PDF](https://wcee.nicee.org/wcee/article/17WCEE/2i-0025.pdf); [EPFL record](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/283792?ln=en). SAHC 2023 analysis chapter: "predicted results varied widely in displacement, interface opening and damage mechanisms, even though material parameters were known. Correct predictions were rare" *(snippet)* — [EPFL record 309533](https://infoscience.epfl.ch/record/309533?ln=fr).
- SERA groin vault (IJAH 2024, 18(12):1825–1851): full-scale cross vault, mortar joints, solid bricks, later TRM-strengthened, horizontal dynamic excitation; "Finite Element Method (FEM) yielded the most accurate predictions regarding displacements and crack patterns"; "a Discrete Element model provided the most accurate prediction of damage mechanisms"; "significant discrepancies in predicted displacements and accelerations" *(snippet)* — [PUCP CRIS](https://cris.pucp.edu.pe/en/publications/shake-table-testing-of-a-brick-masonry-groin-vault-overview-of-bl/).
- PEER 2025 contest (ETH Zürich, NTU Athens, PEER; ERC grant 803908): "it has been shown that it is often impossible to predict the response even of a solitary rocking block. However, it is much easier (and still highly useful) to predict the statistics of the response of a rocking block to a bin of excitations"; 1:15 three-wall masonry models, binder-jet 3D printed with printed notches as joints, tested on a shaking table in the ETH centrifuge (stress similitude; time scale 1/15, acceleration scale 15), "A virgin specimen was used for each excitation", 29 motions; two quantities sought: "The probability of collapse of at least one wall and the displacement of the top of the North wall"; Phase 2 adds ambient-vibration identification data; sampling 4800 Hz, 250 Hz low-pass — [PEER rules PDF](https://peer.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/rules_new.pdf); [PEER announcement](https://peer.berkeley.edu/news/2025-blind-prediction-competition-masonry-structures-now-open).
- Housner-model caveats relevant to rocking predictions: an improved restitution model (Kalliontzis et al. 2016) "is strongly encouraged over Housner's classical one" *(snippet)* — [CROSBI record](https://www.bib.irb.hr/1108475).

### Inferences
- The exercises settle the *mechanism* question better than the *amplitude* question: DE/rigid-block models get the damage mode, continuum FE gets displacements. For a statics engine the actionable lesson is to assert on mechanism and on standing/falling at a given PGA bin, not on a displacement trajectory.
- **Tests to write**
  1. *Rocking-block statistics*: a single block (e.g. 1:4 slenderness) under a bin of ≥ 20 scaled records; assert the overturning fraction, not any single record's outcome (the PEER 2025 framing).
  2. *Aggregate interface opening*: two blocks of walls joined by a weak interface; assert that the interface opens before in-plane failure at low PGA (the AIMS mechanism that participants mostly missed, per the SAHC abstract).

### Gaps
- The numeric spread (ranges of predicted base shear, roof drift, PGA at damage states vs measured) for AIMS was not retrievable — the 17WCEE, CORE and EPFL copies all returned 403/429 during this run.
- The groin-vault paper's numerical discrepancies were not retrieved (abstract only).
- No results exist yet for the PEER 2025 contest (Phase 2 closed July 2025 per the rules; outcome page not found).

### Inferences
- Only Itasca, SimReady, Chrono and MuJoCo publish machine-checkable pass criteria; the Box2D/Bullet/PEEL fixtures are visual regressions and need a displacement/energy criterion added before they can be automated.

### Gaps
- Bullet's own `test/` tree has no stacking regression (only collision, dynamics-pendulum, inverse-dynamics, shared-memory tests were listed); PhysX ships no public unit tests; PEEL's scene list is in an unretrieved manual.
- No code was found that ships a Couplet–Heyman or non-associative-bracket test; those come only from papers.
