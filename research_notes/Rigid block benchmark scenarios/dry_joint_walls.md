# Dry-joint and weakly-bonded masonry walls, panels and piers: published experiments and matching rigid-block limit-analysis results

Scope note for the report writer: every number below was read from a primary or near-primary source that I could open (several open-access PDFs were extracted to text locally with `pdftotext`; the file names of the originals are given in the citations). Where the only accessible copy of a result is a secondary paper that reproduces the original data (notably Bui et al. 2017 reproducing Lourenço 2005 and Restrepo-Vélez 2014, and a 2021 Materials review reproducing Anthoine et al.), that is stated. Units are SI throughout; a few sources give kN/m³ (unit weight) and others kg/m³ (density) — I report each as published and note the conversion only where I combined them. Friction is reported either as a coefficient μ or an angle φ, as the source gave it (μ = tan φ).

## KQ1 — Tilting-table tests on dry-joint masonry (Restrepo-Vélez, Magenes & Griffith 2014; the ROSE/Eucentre report; the Casapulla & Maione corner tests)

### Takeaway
The Pavia programme is 42 configurations of 1:5-scale dry-stacked marble-block walls (façade plus return walls, with/without openings and overburden, one or two storeys) brought to collapse on a tilting platform; the collapse multiplier is λ = tan(θ_cr). The per-configuration table is only in the paywalled IJAH Part I and the 2009 ROSE report, but a 2017 DEM paper reprints the experimental multipliers for eleven of the configurations together with the rigid-block/analytical predictions (D'Ayala 2003, Casapulla 2008, Vaculik 2010): façade-overturning multipliers of 0.21–0.36 for L/H 0.8–1.6, and "mechanism G" (trapezoidal portion with vertical plus diagonal tear lines) multipliers of 0.21–0.35 for L/H 1.1–1.8, with measured block friction coefficients 0.67–0.77.

### Cited Findings

(2014) "Dry Stone Masonry Walls in Bending—Part I: Static Tests", International Journal of Architectural Heritage 8(1):1–28, DOI 10.1080/15583058.2012.663059. "a series of static 1:5-scale tests on dry-stack stone masonry walls … resistance expressed as a collapse load multiplier using an inclined plane machine … The collapse multiplier was calculated from the inclination angle of the platform that caused collapse. In total, 42 configurations were tested, varying the length of the walls, the presence and position of openings, the staggering ratio, the quality of the connection between walls, the existence of vertical overburden loads in the walls, and the number of stories." — [CORE record of Part I](https://core.ac.uk/works/66163003); authorship/DOI confirmed by [Semantic Scholar](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1080/15583058.2012.663059?fields=title,abstract,year,venue,authors,externalIds)

(2020) "Experimental and Analytical Investigation on the Corner Failure in Masonry Buildings: Interaction between Rocking-Sliding and Horizontal Flexure", IJAH 14(2):208–220, DOI 10.1080/15583058.2018.1529206 — [IRIS UniNA record](https://www.iris.unina.it/handle/11588/724517)
- Sensitivity: in the Minho sensitivity study on the Casapulla–Maione corner, the units' Young's modulus "does not influence the results", interface stiffness "affects slightly its final capacity", and the friction angle "has a very significant impact on the structure's global response, resulting in a rise of the load multiplier together with the friction angle values" (angles tried: 0.40, 0.55, 0.60, 0.70 rad). — [Colombo et al. 2021](https://www.canadamasonrydesigncentre.com/wp-content/uploads/14th_symposium/021-Colombo.pdf)

### Inferences
- A known-answer test for this family should pin (i) the multiplier λ = tan θ_cr to within the experimental scatter (≈ ±3 % CV on the corner; S1–S3 repeats spread 0.226–0.254, i.e. ±6 %), (ii) the mechanism class (A/B2 façade rotation about a base hinge with or without a return-wall wedge; G trapezoid bounded by vertical and diagonal tear lines), and (iii) the trend with L/H (λ falls as L/H rises for both families: S8/S9 0.36 at L/H 0.82 → S10 0.21 at 1.63).
- Because the Casapulla upper bound matched and the D'Ayala arching forms over-predicted by 40–67 %, an engine with Coulomb friction but no torsional resistance on bed joints will sit between those two; torsion-shear interaction on the sloped crack is what moves the answer toward the measured value.
- Friction sensitivity is first-order: the measured μ band 0.67–0.77 (±7 % about 0.72) is of the same relative size as the test-to-test scatter, so a benchmark should quote the μ it used and the band.

### Gaps
- The complete 42-row results table (all configurations, openings, overburden, two-storey, staggering ratio) and the actual marble block size and unit weight as reported by Restrepo-Vélez are in the paywalled Part I and in the 36.7 MB Eucentre report; neither was opened. Bui et al.'s "280 mm 800 mm 400 mm" block size is not credible as printed.
- Part II's analytical multipliers per configuration (Vaculik et al. 2014) were not accessible; only the four Vaculik-2010 values reprinted by Bui are reported.
- The Casapulla & Maione corner geometry (block size, courses, wall lengths) is not in the accessible sources; only λ, μ, density and the mechanism are.

## KQ2 — Lourenço, Oliveira, Roca & Orduña (2005) dry-joint stone walls under in-plane compression + shear, and the Orduña & Lourenço (2005) 3-D limit-analysis validations

### Takeaway
The Minho walls are 1.0 × 1.0 × 0.2 m panels of sawn dry stone 0.1 m high × 0.2 m long × 0.2 m thick, held at 30, 100, 200 and 250 kN vertical load (SW30-1/-2, SW100-1/-2, SW200-1/-2, SW250) and pushed laterally through a top beam; failure was a diagonal stepped crack from the loaded top corner with unit crushing at the high loads, measured joint friction 0.62–0.66. The 2-D rigid-block limit analysis of SW30 converges to the asymptote of the FEM curve (≈16 kN); the 3-D Part II paper validates against a 1.2 × 1.0 × 0.2 m dry granite wall at 250 kN (test 86.6 / 92.8 kN, mean 91.6 kN; limit analysis 95.2 kN) and against a hollow pile and two out-of-plane walls (load factors 0.50, ≈0.29).

An embedded concrete beam was placed at the top of the wall where vertical load was assigned at different pre-compression levels: 30 kN (SW30-1 and SW30-2), 100 kN (SW100-1 and SW100-2), 200 kN (SW200-1 and SW200-2) and 250 kN (SW250)"; vertical load applied at 1 kN/s and then held under force control, lateral load by displacement increments; "the friction angle of the joint interface was set to 32° (Lourenço 2005)"; failure "a) de-bonding of the top concrete beam, followed by b) diagonal stepped crack initiated from the top right-hand corner of the wall, with excessive load leading to c) crushing of the stone masonry units"; the DEM matched the SW30 and SW100 load–displacement curves but not SW200/SW250 because it could not crush units. ### Inferences
- The engine test for SW30 should pin: mechanism (stepped diagonal from the loaded top corner, one surviving compression strut, then overturning) and a limit load near 16–17 kN for N = 30 kN with μ ≈ 0.62–0.66 (the LA asymptote of the FEM curve). The joint friction reported for these walls is not single-valued across sources (32° ≈ 0.62 in Bui/Lourenço 2005; 0.66 in Orduña/Oliveira 2003; 0.65 in Vasconcelos 2006 for similar granite), so a ±4 % band on μ is realistic.
- For the Vasconcelos WS.250 wall the limit load (95.2 kN at μ = 0.7, rigid units) exceeds the single-direction peak (86.6 kN) by 10 % but the two-direction mean (91.6 kN) by 4 %; the simple rocking formula with f_c = 73 MPa is an upper bound that the tests approach. A no-crushing engine should expect to land at or slightly above the measured peaks at 250 kN, and closer at 100 kN where rocking is pure.
- The hollow pile is a clean numerical known-answer: with μ = 0.7 the rigid-block answer must fall in [0.427, 0.553] and a non-associative, load-path-following solver gives 0.50.

### Gaps
- The measured peak horizontal loads for SW100, SW200 and SW250 and the authors' own numerical (micro-model) predictions are only in the paywalled JSE paper and Oliveira's 2003 thesis; Bui 2017 shows them only as curves. SW30's experimental peak also appears only graphically (the LA/FEM asymptote ≈16.3 kN is what I can quote).
- Stone density for the Lourenço 2005 walls is not stated in the accessible text (the FEM model used 25 kN/m³).
- Orduña & Lourenço Part I (torsion failure on frictional interfaces) was not opened.

## KQ3 — Casapulla, Portioli, Cascini et al.: rigid-block limit analysis with friction, and the experiments it is validated against

### Takeaway
The Naples group's experimental anchors are (a) two-block tuff stacks loaded eccentrically to map shear–torsion–bending interaction (Casapulla & Portioli 2015), (b) the Casapulla & Maione tilting corner (mean λ = 0.33, μ = 0.62), and (c) the Pavia tilting walls, whose mechanism-G multipliers their torsion-shear macro-block method reproduces as an upper bound; the numerical values accessible to me are those in KQ1. The LP/SOCP formulations (Portioli et al. 2013, 2014) and LiABlock_3D (2020) are cited and described, but their validation tables were not accessible.

### Cited Findings
- Casapulla, C., Portioli, F. (2015) "Experimental and analytical investigation on the frictional contact behavior of 3D masonry block assemblages", Construction and Building Materials 78:126–143, DOI 10.1016/j.conbuildmat.2014.12.100: "extensive experimental investigation on a stack of two dry-jointed tuff blocks subjected to different loading conditions implying interactions among shear, torsion and bending moments", compared with "an analytical model of two overlapped rigid blocks, under assumptions of infinite compressive, shear and tensile strength for blocks and no-tension and frictional behavior at their contact"; the IRIS record carries no file. — [IRIS UniNA](https://www.iris.unina.it/handle/11588/597881); [search summary](https://iris.unina.it/handle/11588/597881)
- Portioli, F., Casapulla, C., Gilbert, M., Cascini, L. - Casapulla, C., Cascini, L., Portioli, F., Landolfo, R. (2014) "3D macro and micro-block models for limit analysis of out-of-plane loaded masonry walls with non-associative Coulomb friction", Meccanica 49(7):1653–1678 — cited by [Bui et al. 2017](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/144603/3/BUI-ALI-VASILIS-MANUSCRIP-REVISED-V17new_Final.pdf); Springer page not fetchable
- Cascini, L., Gagliardo, R., Portioli, F. (2020) "LiABlock_3D: A Software Tool for Collapse Mechanism Analysis of Historic Masonry Structures", IJAH 14(1):75–94: "a rigid block model for collapse mechanism analysis of three-dimensional historic masonry structures subjected to point live loads, seismic-induced lateral loads and settlements, made of polyhedral rigid blocks interacting at no-tension, frictional contact interfaces … Numerical and experimental case studies from the literature were analyzed … arches, vaults, and domes under vertical and horizontal live loads and spreading supports." — [search summary of IRIS record](https://www.iris.unina.it/handle/11588/838331)
- Casapulla (2008) analytical procedure "to evaluate upper and lower thresholds for the possible collapse load factor for in-plane and out-of-plane loaded block masonry structures with frictional resistance … A particular class of mechanisms characterized by out-of-plane torsion-shear interactions on frictional interfaces"; applied to Pavia mechanism-G tests, its upper bound "provides a good agreement with the numerical results" while the lower bound "underestimates the results obtained from the experiment" (numbers in KQ1). — [Bui et al. 2017](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/144603/3/BUI-ALI-VASILIS-MANUSCRIP-REVISED-V17new_Final.pdf)
- Casapulla & Maione corner: five tilting tests, mean λ = 0.33 (CV 2.7 %), μ = 0.62, ρ = 1.23 t/m³; "three-dimensional macro-block model with frictional joints", with "frictional resistances evaluated for the rocking-sliding mechanism while simplified yield conditions were adopted for the torsion strength in the horizontal flexure mechanism". — [Colombo et al. 2021](https://www.canadamasonrydesigncentre.com/wp-content/uploads/14th_symposium/021-Colombo.pdf); [IRIS record](https://www.iris.unina.it/handle/11588/724517)

### Inferences
- The Naples benchmarks that an engine can actually reproduce from accessible data are the corner (λ = 0.33 ± 0.01 with μ = 0.62) and the Pavia S-series via Bui's reprint; the two-block torsion tests would be the right place to validate a joint's combined shear–torsion yield surface, but their numbers are behind the paywall.
- Because the group's own comparison shows the torsion-free D'Ayala forms overshooting by 40–67 % on façade mechanisms while the torsion-shear bound lands within a few percent, a test on mechanism G is effectively a test of whether bed-joint torsional friction is represented.

### Gaps
- Tuff block size, weight, friction coefficient, eccentricities and measured vs analytical collapse loads for Casapulla & Portioli 2015 — not accessible (closed access, no repository file).
- Which specific experiments the 2013/2014 LP/SOCP papers and LiABlock_3D use for validation, and their numbers — not accessible (ctresources page errored; Springer/Elsevier pages blocked).

## KQ4 — Giuffrè / D'Ayala & Speranza (FaMIVE) closed-form multipliers for façade, return-wall and corner mechanisms, and how tilting tests compared

### Takeaway
D'Ayala & Speranza (2003) define the FaMIVE mechanism catalogue (A: free façade overturning; B1/B2: overturning dragging one or both return-wall wedges; …; G: trapezoid with vertical and diagonal tear lines) whose multipliers are closed-form functions of thickness/height, overlap and friction. I could not open a source that prints the formulas; what is documented is how they compared to the Pavia tilting tests: 0.438–0.545 predicted against 0.29–0.36 measured for S7–S9 and 0.356 against 0.213 for S10 (i.e. 40–67 % high), while Vaculik's (2010) formulation lands within 0–11 % of the measurements.

### Cited Findings
- Citation and scope: D'Ayala, D., Speranza, E. (2003) "Definition of collapse mechanisms and seismic vulnerability of historic masonry buildings", Earthquake Spectra 19(3):479–509 — in the reference list of [Colombo et al. 2021](https://www.canadamasonrydesigncentre.com/wp-content/uploads/14th_symposium/021-Colombo.pdf). The FaMIVE procedure "comprehensively accounts for all possible in plane (IP), out-of-plane (OOP), and combined (COM) failure mechanisms" and identifies "the multiplier factor of the main four local collapse mechanisms (overturning, horizontal arch effect, vertical arch effect and corner overturning)" — [UCL EPICentre FaMIVE page / search summary](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/epicentre/news/2024/apr/famive-recent-developments-and-applications)
- Mechanism definitions as reproduced: "The type A mechanism corresponds to the overturning of the façade wall when there is poor or no connection with the orthogonal walls … The type B2 mechanism occurs instead of type A mechanism when the level of connection is sufficient to involve, beyond the main wall, one or both partition walls in the overturning, due to a sufficient length of overlapping … developed by the occurrence of a diagonal crack along the partition walls and a horizontal hinge on the façade"; mechanism G: "a trapezoidal central portion with two tear lines: vertical and diagonal cracking". D'Ayala's model assumes "dry block masonry connected together by pure friction and cohesion". — [Bui et al. 2017](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/144603/3/BUI-ALI-VASILIS-MANUSCRIP-REVISED-V17new_Final.pdf)
- Measured vs D'Ayala-2003 vs Vaculik-2010 multipliers for the Pavia façade tests (A/B2): S7 (L/H 1.088) 0.291 vs 0.438 vs 0.273; S8 (0.816) 0.362 vs 0.506 vs 0.352; S9 (0.816) 0.352 vs 0.545 vs 0.352; S10 (1.633) 0.213 vs 0.356 vs 0.190; D'Ayala differences −50.5 %, −39.8 %, −54.8 %, −67.1 %; Vaculik differences 6.2 %, 2.8 %, 0.0 %, 10.8 %. For mechanism G the D'Ayala values "have large deviations from the experimental results since torsional friction along the sloped crack line is not taken into account". — [Bui et al. 2017](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/144603/3/BUI-ALI-VASILIS-MANUSCRIP-REVISED-V17new_Final.pdf)
- Parametric kinematic (virtual-work) evaluation of the same four local mechanisms as functions of wall slenderness h/t and length L, for masonries of specific weight 11, 16 and 22 kN/m³, thicknesses 0.30–0.80 m, storey heights 3–5 m, 1–3 storeys — design charts only, no closed forms printed in the text. — Formisano, Chieffo, Milo, Fabbrocino, "The influence of local mechanisms on large scale seismic vulnerability estimation of masonry building aggregates", [IRIS UniNA PDF](https://www.iris.unina.it/retrieve/e268a72f-5ed3-4c8f-e053-1705fe0a812c/9_Formisano_ICCMSE.pdf)
- Corner mechanism measured value for comparison: Casapulla & Maione mean λ = 0.33 with μ = 0.62 (details in KQ1). — [Colombo et al. 2021](https://www.canadamasonrydesigncentre.com/wp-content/uploads/14th_symposium/021-Colombo.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a free-standing rigid wall of thickness t and height h rotating about its toe under a horizontal body force λW, moment equilibrium gives λ = t/h (centroid at mid-thickness, mid-height); this is the mechanism-A base case and is what the engine should return exactly for a single-leaf wall with no return and no overburden. Return walls and overburden raise λ; the Pavia S-series values (0.21–0.36 for 1:5 walls) are the measured values of the with-returns cases. (Derived, not quoted from a source.)
- The 40–67 % over-prediction of the arching-based D'Ayala forms against tilting tests, versus ≤11 % for Vaculik and a few percent for DEM/torsion-shear bounds, is the most useful calibration fact: an engine should not be validated against the D'Ayala closed forms for return-wall mechanisms, only against the measured multipliers.

### Gaps
- The explicit D'Ayala & Speranza (2003) and Giuffrè formulas for mechanisms A, B1, B2, corner, with their symbols (overlap length, friction, γ) — no open copy found (Earthquake Spectra paywalled; the TU Delft and Rijeka pages I tried returned 404). The Formisano paper has only charts.
- Giuffrè's original texts (1991/1993, in Italian) were not searched for directly.

## KQ5 — Rocking and shear strength of URM piers: Magenes & Calvi (1997) closed forms and representative tests

### Takeaway
The Magenes–Calvi rocking strength is V_r = (l² t σ₀ / 2 H₀)(1 − σ₀/(0.85 f_c)) (shear span H₀ = h for a cantilever, h/2 for fixed–fixed), with a Mann–Müller-type stepped-sliding criterion for shear. Representative tests: the Ispra/Pavia brick walls (1.0 m long, 1.35 m and 2.0 m high, σ₀ = 0.60 MPa) reached 75 kN (diagonal stepped sliding) and 65 kN (flexural/rocking); the Minho dry granite walls (1.2 × 1.0 × 0.2 m) rocked at 37–41 kN under 0.5 MPa and 86–93 kN under 1.25 MPa with toe crushing.

### Cited Findings
- Citation: Magenes, G., Calvi, G.M. (1997) "In-plane seismic response of brick masonry walls", Earthquake Engineering and Structural Dynamics 26:1091–1112 — reference list of [Vasconcelos & Lourenço 2006](https://repositorium.uminho.pt/server/api/core/bitstreams/9b4098a4-342f-4b9b-b49c-08c1f155334b/content); volume/issue/pages also in [search summary](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11831-019-09351-x)
- Rocking (flexural) strength, rectangular stress block with 0.85 reduction: "H_r = (l² t σ / 2h) (1 − σ/(0.85 f_c)) where f_c is the compressive strength of the masonry … l, t and h are respectively the length, thickness and height of the wall. The vertical stress, σ, is calculated based on the cross section of the wall." — [Vasconcelos & Lourenço 2006, Eq. (1)](https://repositorium.uminho.pt/server/api/core/bitstreams/9b4098a4-342f-4b9b-b49c-08c1f155334b/content)
- Same formula in Magenes–Calvi notation as reprinted in a 2021 review: flexural "V_MC,f = B s σ₀/(2ψλ) · (1 − σ₀/(0.85 f_c,av))" (B length, s thickness, λ aspect ratio H/B, ψ = 1 cantilever, 0.5 double-fixed) and diagonal stepped-crack sliding "V_MC,dss = B s (1.5 f′_v0 + μ′σ₀)/(1 + 3 f′_v0 ψλ/σ₀)". — [Literature Review of the In-Plane Behavior of Masonry Walls, Materials 2021, PMC8200034](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8200034/)
- Ispra/Pavia brick wall tests (Anthoine et al., cited there as 1994): "Case 1-R: 1.0 m × 1.35 m height, axial stress 0.60 MPa, lateral strength 75.0 kN, failure DSS (diagonal sliding shear)"; "Case 2-R: 1.0 m × 2.0 m height, axial stress 0.60 MPa, lateral strength 65.0 kN, failure flexural". — [PMC8200034](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8200034/) (secondary reprint; the primary is Anthoine, Magonette, Magenes 1995, 10th ECEE, "Shear-compression testing and analysis of brick masonry walls", vol. 302 p. 175 per [search summary](https://peer.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/componenttesting.pdf))
- Dry granite pier rocking data (full table in KQ2): WS.100 36.9/−40.7 kN, WS.175 62.8/−67.8 kN, WS.250 86.3/−93.0 kN at σ = 0.5/0.875/1.25 MPa; "The lateral resistance obtained in shear tests is remarkably close to the flexural resistance calculated for a compressive strength of f_c = 73.0 N/mm2. However, flexural response appears to be an upper bound". — [Vasconcelos & Lourenço 2006](https://repositorium.uminho.pt/server/api/core/bitstreams/9b4098a4-342f-4b9b-b49c-08c1f155334b/content)
- Limit-analysis rocking on the Vasconcelos wall at 250 kN with the load line 0.125 m above the top: 95.2 kN (μ = 0.7, infinite f_c) vs 86.6/92.8 kN measured. — [Orduña & Lourenço 2005 Part II](https://repositorium.uminho.pt/bitstreams/1b6aa5d7-2879-4a6f-a6d5-6635709a3e0f/download)
- Minho dry-joint wall in differential settlement (a third pier-scale dry-joint benchmark): three 1,575 × 1,000 × 220 mm single-leaf dry-stacked brick panels (20 courses, bricks 50 × 105 × 220 mm, 2,200 kg/m³, measured friction angle 38°) settled at one end at 2 mm/s up to 140 mm; failure "characterized by two major cracks … at the centre of the wall … from bending … [and] at the left-hand side and at the bottom … resulting in the formation of a brick-stacked pyramid"; qualitative only (no force record). — [Bui et al. 2017](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/144603/3/BUI-ALI-VASILIS-MANUSCRIP-REVISED-V17new_Final.pdf)

### Inferences
- Numerical check of the closed form against the dry granite wall: with l = 1.0 m, t = 0.2 m, N = 100 kN (σ = 0.5 MPa), cantilever H₀ = 1.2 m + 0.125 m load offset ≈ 1.325 m and f_c = 73 MPa, H_r = (1.0² × 0.2 × 0.5e6 / (2 × 1.325)) × (1 − 0.5/62) ≈ 37.4 kN, against 36.9/40.7 kN measured; at 250 kN it gives ≈ 92.4 kN against 86.3/93.0 kN — so the engine's rigid-block rocking answer (f_c = ∞) for these walls is N·l/(2H₀) and should sit within ~5 % of the measured means at 0.5 MPa.
- A pier test should pin two things: the regime boundary (rocking vs stepped sliding, which the Ispra pair at the same σ₀ shows flipping between h = 1.35 and 2.0 m) and the rocking force N·l/(2H₀) corrected by (1 − σ₀/0.85 f_c) if crushing is modelled.

### Gaps
- Magenes & Calvi's own 1997 text (and the exact Pavia 1992 cyclic test numbers) was not opened; the 75/65 kN values come from a 2021 review table citing Anthoine et al.
- Brick/mortar unit weight and the shear-strength parameters (f′_v0, μ′) of the Ispra walls were not in the accessible excerpt.

## KQ6 — Dry-stone retaining walls: Burgoyne 1834 (Kingstown), Harkness et al. 2000 UDEC, Villemus et al. 2007, Mundell et al. 2009/2010 (Bath)

### Takeaway
Burgoyne's four 6.1 m granite walls (equal volume, different sections) are the canonical known-answer: A (1.02 m uniform, battered 1:5) and B (1.63→0.41 m, vertical back) stood to full height (B moved 63 mm), C (same as B but vertical face, batter at the rear) and D (1.02 m uniform, vertical) collapsed at ≈5.2 m of fill — C by bursting/bulging at 1.5–1.7 m, D by toppling from the base; UDEC with γ_granite 2270 kg/m³, block friction 45°, loose fill 1390–1550 kg/m³ and φ 28° reproduced the stable/failed split and the failure height. ### Inferences
- Burgoyne's set is a clean four-way discriminator for a rigid-block engine with a soil-pressure driver: with γ_wall = 2270 kg/m³ (22.3 kN/m³), block μ = tan 45° = 1.0, fill 1450–1550 kg/m³ and φ = 28° (K_a-type active pressure with full wall friction), the engine should keep A and B standing at 6.1 m of fill and bring C and D down at ≈5.2 m, with D rotating about the toe (resultant through the toe) and C failing internally at 1.5–1.7 m height. The 63 mm top movement of B (vs 32.6 mm in UDEC) says the block-interface stiffness, not the strength, is the loosest parameter.
- TRL's sensitivity runs say the block-to-block friction coefficient matters little for these walls (sliding was small relative to toe rotation) while a vertical joint parallel to the face can switch a stable wall to overturning — the structural parameter to test is the bond pattern, not μ.
- Bath walls 1 and 2 are toppling cases whose predicted/observed surcharge ratios (1.18 and 0.71) bracket the monolithic limit-equilibrium answer; walls 3 and 4 (voidage 44–46 %, small rounded stones) fail by internal rotation/bulge that a monolith cannot represent — a discrete engine that lets blocks rotate individually is the point.

### Gaps
- Villemus et al.'s per-wall numbers (heights 2–4.25 m, thickness, stone type—limestone vs schist—unit weight, interface friction angle, water height at yield, internal failure angle) are only in the paywalled paper; the HAL copy was blocked by an anti-bot wall.
- Harkness et al. 2000's own text (and its DOI) was not opened; the ICE page returned 403. The UDEC parameters above come from TRL550, written by two of the same project's authors and explicitly reporting Harkness et al.'s runs.
- Bath: the individual stone unit weight (as opposed to the 24.5 kN/m³ "wall material density" with 23–46 % voidage), block–block friction measurements, and the fifth wall's results were not in the two accessible papers.

## What a test should pin, and friction sensitivity (cross-cutting)

### Takeaway
Across all families the measured quantity is either a dimensionless multiplier (λ = tan θ_cr, or a_cr/g) or a force at fixed axial load; mechanisms fall into five classes an engine must be able to produce — toe-hinge overturning (λ ≈ t/h for a free wall; 0.21–0.36 for the Pavia façades), return-wall wedges and corner mixed modes (0.33), stepped diagonal in-plane cracking with a single compression strut, pier rocking (N·l/2H₀), and internal bulge/burst in thick dry-stone walls — and friction governs the second and third classes strongly, the first and fourth barely.

### Cited Findings
- Friction is the dominant parameter for corner/wedge mechanisms (Minho sensitivity, friction angle 0.40–0.70 rad) — [Colombo et al. 2021](https://www.canadamasonrydesigncentre.com/wp-content/uploads/14th_symposium/021-Colombo.pdf); the measured μ band for the Pavia blocks was 0.67–0.77 — [Bui et al. 2017](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/144603/3/BUI-ALI-VASILIS-MANUSCRIP-REVISED-V17new_Final.pdf)
- Friction barely matters for toe-overturning of thick retaining walls: "sliding movements between the blocks were relatively small compared to those generated by overturning of the base about its toe" (block φ = 45° baseline, varied in later runs) — [TRL550](https://www.stonewalls.ch/system/files/2025-04/2002%20Analysis%20stability%20masonry%20retaining%20walls.pdf); "resistance to sliding is generally very high for most walling materials, it is rotation rather than translation that causes the development of bulges" — [Mundell 2010](https://purehost.bath.ac.uk/ws/files/305502/stbu163-003.pdf)
- Apparent vs joint friction in piers: joint μ = 0.65 (dry granite) but the wall-strength envelope fits a Coulomb line of "approximately 0.4 in dry walls" because rocking, not sliding, governs — [Vasconcelos & Lourenço 2006](https://repositorium.uminho.pt/server/api/core/bitstreams/9b4098a4-342f-4b9b-b49c-08c1f155334b/content)
- Non-associativity matters for the number, not just the mechanism: minimising the load factor under zero dilatancy gave 0.427 vs 0.500 (pile) and 0.193 vs 0.285 (two-edge wall), i.e. 15–32 % lower than the load-path solution, and "unrealistically small … 40%" for the one-edge wall — [Orduña & Lourenço 2005 Part II](https://repositorium.uminho.pt/bitstreams/1b6aa5d7-2879-4a6f-a6d5-6635709a3e0f/download)

### Inferences
- Recommended known-answer set, in order of how tightly the answer is pinned: (1) single granite block tan α (0.25/0.17/0.12) and Burgoyne A–D stand/fall at 6.1 vs 5.2 m; (2) Vasconcelos WS.100 rocking at 37–41 kN and WS.250 at 86–93 kN; (3) Casapulla–Maione corner λ = 0.33 ± 0.01 at μ = 0.62; (4) Pavia S7–S10 façades 0.29/0.36/0.35/0.21 and S1–S6 mechanism-G 0.21–0.35 at μ = 0.67–0.77; (5) Orduña pile 0.50 ∈ [0.427, 0.553] and two-edge wall 0.26–0.29 as pure numerical cross-checks; (6) Bath wall 1 toppling at ≈110 kN surcharge with 3.75° base tilt as a soil-coupled stretch case.
- Where a test's answer depends on μ (classes 2–3), the test should be run at the ends of the published band and assert monotonic increase of λ with μ, since every source that varied μ reports that monotonicity and none gives a closed form.

### Gaps
- No source in this pass gives a published dλ/dμ (or dλ/dφ) table for any wall benchmark; sensitivity is reported qualitatively or by curves only.
